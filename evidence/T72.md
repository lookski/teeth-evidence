# T72. Mouthwash and oral cancer: 40% higher risk in men, 60% in women (1991 case-control)

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 06-hot-topics](../topics/06-hot-topics.md) · `T72`

## Bottom line

Interviews with **866 patients** with cancer of the oral cavity and pharynx and **1249 controls**
of similar age and sex from four areas of the United States found **increased risks associated with
regular mouthwash use**: risks of oral cancer were elevated by **40% among male** and **60% among
female** mouthwash users, **after adjusting for tobacco and alcohol consumption**. This is the
foundational study behind the "mouthwash causes oral cancer" scare — an adjusted observational
association in a large case-control sample, not a causal demonstration.

## Study designs and populations

- Design: case-control study
- Cases: **866** patients with cancer of the oral cavity and pharynx
- Controls: **1249** controls of similar age and sex, from the general population in **four areas of
  the United States**
- Exposure: regular mouthwash use (assessed by interview)
- Adjustment: **tobacco and alcohol consumption** — the two dominant confounders for oral cancer
- Outcome: oral and pharyngeal cancer risk

## Key numbers (verbatim from abstract)

| Group | Excess risk associated with regular mouthwash use |
|---|---|
| **Men** | **40% higher** (after adjusting for tobacco and alcohol) |
| **Women** | **60% higher** (after adjusting for tobacco and alcohol) |
| Cases | 866 |
| Controls | 1249 |

- The abstract reports the excess as percentages, **not** as odds ratios with confidence intervals —
  for the exact ORs and CIs, 以原文为准

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- The direction is consistent with later work (see T73): frequent mouthwash use is associated with
  higher upper-aerodigestive cancer risk
- The critical caveat: mouthwash use correlates strongly with **oral hygiene behaviour, dental
  visiting, smoking and alcohol** — adjusting for tobacco and alcohol helps but cannot fully remove
  confounding, especially since heavy mouthwash users may differ in unmeasured ways
- Alcohol-containing mouthwash is the biologically plausible culprit (acetaldehyde exposure), which
  is why the association is not dismissed outright; see T73 for the ADH7 effect-modification signal
  that supports this mechanism
- Practical: this does not mean occasional mouthwash is dangerous. The signal is about **regular,
  frequent, long-term use**, and it is strongest for alcohol-containing products

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: large case-control sample (866 cases, 1249 controls); population-based controls from
  four regions; adjustment for the two biggest confounders (tobacco, alcohol); sex-specific estimates
  reported
- Limitations: **observational case-control** — recall bias on mouthwash use is plausible; no
  dose-response quantification in the abstract; 1991 data, when mouthwash formulations and use
  patterns differed from today; residual confounding by oral hygiene and socioeconomic status cannot
  be excluded; **no information on alcohol content** of the products used
- This study alone cannot establish causation, and it should never be cited as if it did

## Consistency with other evidence

- Consistent in direction with T73 (ARCAGE: mouthwash **>=3 times/day** OR **3.23, 95% CI 1.68-6.19**),
  with a plausible alcohol/acetaldehyde mechanism supported by ADH7 effect modification there
- Consistent with T74's review framing (at least 10 case-control studies over three decades)
- The three together establish "repeated association", not "proven carcinogen"
- Cross-check with T75: antibacterial mouthwash also raises blood pressure — a separate, better
  controlled harm signal that does not depend on confounding
