# T64. Prenatal fluoride and cognition (Mexico): urinary fluoride +0.5 mg/L = 3.15 lower GCI, 2.50 lower IQ

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 06-hot-topics](../topics/06-hot-topics.md) · `T64`

## Bottom line

In **299 mother-child pairs** in Mexico with individual urinary fluoride measurements, a **0.5 mg/L
increase in maternal urinary fluoride** (approximately the interquartile range) predicted **3.15
lower offspring General Cognitive Index at age 4 (95% CI -5.42 to -0.87)** and **2.50 lower IQ at
age 6-12 (95% CI -4.12 to -0.59)**. Mean maternal urinary fluoride was **0.90 (0.35) mg/L** and
children's was **0.82 (0.38) mg/L** — i.e. in the **general range** reported for other general
populations, not an industrial high-exposure setting.

## Study designs and populations

- Design: prospective mother-child cohort (ELEMENT study, Mexico City)
- Sample: **299** mother-child pairs with complete data; **287** had General Cognitive Index (GCI)
  data and **211** had IQ data
- Exposure: fluoride measured in **archived maternal urine samples taken during pregnancy** and in
  children's urine at age 6-12, by ion-selective electrode; adjusted for creatinine and specific
  gravity respectively
- Outcomes: GCI of the McCarthy Scales of Children's Abilities at **age 4**; full-scale IQ from the
  Wechsler Abbreviated Scale of Intelligence at **age 6-12**
- Mean (SD) urinary fluoride: mothers **0.90 (0.35) mg/L** (n=299); children **0.82 (0.38) mg/L** (n=211)

## Key numbers (verbatim from abstract)

| Comparison | Result (95% CI) |
|---|---|
| **Maternal urine fluoride +0.5 mg/L -> GCI at age 4** | **-3.15 (-5.42 to -0.87)** |
| **Maternal urine fluoride +0.5 mg/L -> IQ at age 6-12** | **-2.50 (-4.12 to -0.59)** |
| Mean (SD) maternal urinary fluoride | **0.90 (0.35) mg/L** |
| Mean (SD) child urinary fluoride | **0.82 (0.38) mg/L** |
| Pairs with complete data | **299** (GCI n=287; IQ n=211) |

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- Two outcomes, two ages, both significant and in the same direction — this is what makes T64
  stronger than a single-endpoint result
- Effect magnitude is modest: 2.5-3.15 IQ points per 0.5 mg/L, i.e. roughly 0.2 standard deviations
- **Key contextual fact**: the studied exposure range (mean 0.90 mg/L maternal urinary fluoride) is
  described in the abstract as the **general range reported for other general population samples of
  pregnant women and nonpregnant adults** — so this is not a factory-town study
- Practical: adds weight to avoiding **excess ingested fluoride in pregnancy**; it does not by itself
  quantify a safe water fluoride concentration, because urinary fluoride integrates all sources
  (water, tea, toothpaste ingestion, food)

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: **individual biomarker** of exposure (not ecological water level); longitudinal with two
  cognitive endpoints at two ages; prospective prenatal sampling; exposure range representative of
  general populations
- Limitations: observational; residual confounding by lead, mercury, iodine, maternal education and
  nutrition is plausible; **211** children for the IQ analysis is modest; single cohort in one city;
  urinary fluoride is a short-half-life marker influenced by hydration and renal function; no
  randomisation, so causality is not established
- Same class of caveat as T63: signal, not settled toxicology

## Consistency with other evidence

- Consistent with T63 (Canada: 1 mg/L MUFSG = -4.49 IQ in boys, -3.66 per mg/day intake both sexes)
  — independent cohorts, same direction, comparable magnitude per unit exposure
- Consistent with T65 (formula-fed infants: larger effect where water fluoride is concentrated in
  formula) — a plausible mechanism for higher exposure in a susceptible window
- Tension with T61/T62 dental benefits: the two literatures answer different questions
  (caries prevention vs neurodevelopment) and should be presented as a balance, not a winner
- Note the contested replication status of this literature — cite as a risk signal requiring
  cautious interpretation, not as proof
