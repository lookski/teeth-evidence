# T87. Oral microbiome and systemic disease: dysbiosis is associated with far more than the mouth

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 06-hot-topics](../topics/06-hot-topics.md) · `T87`

## Bottom line

A 2024 review describes the oral microbiome as "a complex ecosystem consisting of **bacteria, fungi,
archaea, and viruses**" whose balance is disturbed by **diet, smoking, alcohol consumption, lifestyle
choices, and medical conditions**, producing **dysbiosis** linked to **dental caries, gingivitis,
periodontitis, oral candidiasis and halitosis**. It then reviews "novel associations" with **systemic
diseases including gastrointestinal, cardiovascular, endocrinal and neurological conditions,
autoimmune diseases, and cancer**, and assesses interventions — **dental probiotics, xylitol, oral
rinses, fluoride, essential oils, oil pulling and peptides** — for their ability to modulate the
microbiome.

## Study designs and populations

- Design: **narrative review** (2024)
- Scope: oral microbiome composition, functions, influencing factors, links to oral and systemic
  disease, and personalised oral care strategies
- Organisms covered: **bacteria, fungi, archaea, viruses**
- Modifiable influences named: **diet, smoking, alcohol, lifestyle choices, medical conditions**
- Oral conditions linked to dysbiosis: **caries, gingivitis, periodontitis, oral candidiasis, halitosis**
- Systemic conditions reviewed: **gastrointestinal, cardiovascular, endocrinal, neurological,
  autoimmune diseases, cancer**
- Interventions reviewed: **dental probiotics, xylitol, oral rinses, fluoride, essential oils,
  oil pulling, peptides**

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- The review's value is **scope, not quantification**: it maps the claim "oral health is systemic
  health" onto specific disease categories, and lists the modifiable levers
- Note that the same intervention list appears elsewhere in this project with actual data: **fluoride**
  (T15), **oil pulling** (T58-T60, T77-T78), **oral rinses** (T72-T76), **xylitol** (T22-T26 cluster)
  — this review is the connective tissue, not the evidence
- "Novel associations" is the operative phrase: most systemic links are **associational**, and this
  project's own T84 shows how badly such associations can be confounded (HR 0.2 for flossing)
- Practical: the actionable core is unglamorous — control plaque, don't smoke, limit sugar, keep
  saliva flowing. Microbiome-modulating products are mostly unproven adjuvants

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: unusually broad scope (four microbial kingdoms, six systemic disease categories, seven
  intervention classes); up to date (2024); names modifiable factors explicitly
- Limitations: **narrative review** — no systematic search, no quality appraisal, no pooled effect
  sizes; abstract reports **no numbers at all**; "novel associations" for systemic disease are
  presented without strength-of-evidence grading; cannot be cited for any quantitative claim
- Use as a map, never as proof of any specific association

## Consistency with other evidence

- Consistent with T88 (Nature Reviews Microbiology: biogeography and immune interaction, systemic
  health role) and T89 (Periodontology 2000: key organisms and networks) — three independent reviews
  converge on the same framing
- Consistent with this project's specific mechanism entries: T76 (oral bacteria are necessary for
  dietary-nitrate conversion, so antiseptic mouthwash raises blood pressure) is a concrete example of
  the mouth-systemic axis
- **Caution** from within this project: T84 (interdental cleaning and cardiovascular events, HR 0.2)
  shows how easily systemic associations become implausible; treat "oral microbiome affects systemic
  disease" as a promising research area, not a clinical promise
- Consistent with T58-T60/T77-T78 on oil pulling: the review lists it among interventions, but the
  outcome data in this project show only short-term antiplaque effects
