# T63. Prenatal fluoride and child IQ (Canada): 1 mg/L higher maternal urinary fluoride = 4.49 lower IQ in boys

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 06-hot-topics](../topics/06-hot-topics.md) · `T63`

## Bottom line

In **601 mother-child pairs** from 6 Canadian cities (children born 2008-2012, IQ tested at 3-4 y),
a **1 mg/L increase in maternal urinary fluoride (MUFSG)** was associated with a **4.49-point lower
IQ (95% CI -8.38 to -0.60) in boys**, with no significant association in girls (B = 2.40, 95% CI
-2.53 to 7.33); the sex interaction was significant (**P = .02**). A **1 mg higher daily fluoride
intake** was associated with **3.66 lower IQ (95% CI -7.16 to -0.14)** across both sexes. Women in
fluoridated areas had MUFSG 0.69 mg/L vs 0.40 mg/L in non-fluoridated areas (P = .001).

## Study designs and populations

- Design: prospective mother-child cohort (Maternal-Infant Research on Environmental Chemicals, MIREC)
- Sample: **601 mother-child pairs** from 6 major Canadian cities
- Children born 2008-2012; IQ assessed at ages 3-4 y (Wechsler Primary and Preschool Scale of
  Intelligence-III, Full Scale IQ)
- Exposure 1: maternal urinary fluoride adjusted for specific gravity, averaged across 3 trimesters
  (available for **512** pregnant women)
- Exposure 2: self-reported maternal daily fluoride intake from water and beverages
  (available for **400** pregnant women)
- **41%** lived in communities with fluoridated municipal water
- Maternal characteristics: mean (SD) age 32.3 (5.1) y; 463 (90%) white; 264 children (52%) female
- Analysis window: March 2017 to January 2019

## Key numbers (verbatim from abstract)

| Comparison | Result (95% CI) |
|---|---|
| MUFSG, fluoridated (n=141) vs non-fluoridated (n=228) areas | **0.69 (0.42) vs 0.40 (0.27) mg/L**, P = .001 |
| Daily fluoride intake, fluoridated vs non-fluoridated | **0.93 (0.43) vs 0.30 (0.26) mg/day**, P = .001 |
| **MUFSG +1 mg/L -> IQ in boys** | **-4.49 (-8.38 to -0.60)** |
| MUFSG +1 mg/L -> IQ in girls | +2.40 (-2.53 to 7.33), **not significant** |
| Sex x MUFSG interaction | **P = .02 (6.89; 95% CI 0.96-12.82)** |
| **Daily fluoride intake +1 mg -> IQ (both sexes)** | **-3.66 (-7.16 to -0.14)** |
| Mean (SD) Full Scale IQ of children | 107.16 (13.26), range 52-143 |
| Girls vs boys mean IQ | 109.56 (11.96) vs 104.61 (14.09), P = .001 |

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- The exposure contrast is real but small: fluoridated-area mothers averaged 0.69 mg/L urinary
  fluoride vs 0.40 in non-fluoridated areas — a 0.29 mg/L difference, well under the 1 mg/L unit
  used in the effect estimate
- The sex-specific finding (boys affected, girls not) is the most fragile part: it rests on an
  interaction test at P = .02 with no clear mechanism, and it is not replicated consistently (see T64)
- Absolute effect size context: IQ mean was 107, SD 13.26 — a 4.49-point drop is about one-third
  of a standard deviation, at the upper end of what epidemiology usually detects
- Practical: this is an association in a single cohort, not a randomised comparison; it should inform
  **pregnancy** risk-benefit discussion, not be used to declare water fluoridation harmful to
  children's teeth (a different exposure window and route)

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: prospective prenatal exposure measurement (urinary biomarker, not ecological water
  level); IQ tested with a standardised instrument; both a biomarker and a self-reported intake
  analysis pointing the same direction; dose-response reported
- Limitations: **observational** — residual confounding by maternal nutrition, income, education,
  and other neurotoxicants cannot be excluded; single country and 90% white sample; IQ at 3-4 y is
  an early, unstable endpoint; sex-specific effect lacks a mechanism; urinary fluoride reflects
  recent intake and is confounded by hydration and renal function
- The paper's own conclusion is phrased as "associated with", not causal

## Consistency with other evidence

- Consistent in direction with T64 (Mexico: maternal urinary fluoride +0.5 mg/L predicted 3.15 lower
  GCI and 2.50 lower IQ) — two independent cohorts, similar magnitude
- Consistent with T65 (infant formula and water fluoride: 0.5 mg/L higher water fluoride = 9.3-point
  lower Performance IQ in formula-fed children) — larger effect where formula concentrates exposure
- Tension with T61/T62: those establish the **dental** benefit of fluoridation. Both can be true;
  the question is the risk-benefit balance, and these three entries are the harm side of that ledger
- Methodological caution: a 2024-2025 replication literature exists and is contested — treat
  T63-T65 as "signal requiring replication", not settled toxicology
