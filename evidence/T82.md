# T82. Interdental brushes beat floss in untreated periodontitis: plaque 2.15 vs 2.47 at 6 weeks

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 06-hot-topics](../topics/06-hot-topics.md) · `T82`

## Bottom line

In **26 untreated patients** with moderate to severe periodontitis (mean age 37.4, range 27-72),
a split-mouth 6-week comparison of **dental floss (DF) on one side vs interdental brushes (IDB) on
the other** found: approximal plaque fell from **3.09 to 2.15** with IDB and from **3.10 to 2.47**
with DF — **IDB removed more approximal plaque**. Probing depth started at 5.84 mm (IDB sites) and
5.59 mm (DF sites) and fell to **5.01 mm at 6 weeks for both**. So IDB wins on plaque, ties on
probing depth.

## Study designs and populations

- Design: **split-mouth** comparison (each patient used DF on one side, IDB on the other)
- Sample: **26 patients** (12 female, 14 male), mean age **37.4 years**, range 27-72
- Condition: **untreated** moderate to severe periodontitis
- Duration: **6 weeks**, prior to subgingival debridement
- Adjunct to: daily toothbrushing
- Instructions: oral hygiene instruction for brushing and both devices at baseline and **week 3**
- Outcomes: plaque scores, **probing depth**, and **two bleeding scores** (periodontal pocket bleeding
  index and angulated bleeding index)

| **Approximal plaque, baseline -> 6 weeks** | **3.09 -> 2.15** | **3.10 -> 2.47** |
| Probing depth, baseline | 5.84 mm | 5.59 mm |
| Probing depth, 6 weeks | **5.01 mm** | **5.01 mm** |

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- The plaque difference is meaningful: IDB achieved a **0.94-point** drop vs DF's **0.63-point** drop
  from a nearly identical baseline (3.09 vs 3.10) — a clean comparison because of the split-mouth design
- **Probing depth improvement was identical (both 5.01 mm)** — neither device reduced pocket depth
  beyond what a 6-week hygiene phase achieves; depth reduction needs debridement
- Practical: for periodontitis patients with open embrasures, **interdental brushes are the better
  tool**; floss is for tight contacts where a brush will not pass
- Split-mouth design is a real strength here: patient-level hygiene behaviour is controlled because
  the same person uses both devices

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: **split-mouth design** removes between-patient confounding; **untreated periodontitis**
  population (a clinically relevant, under-studied group); two bleeding indices plus plaque and
  probing depth; 6-week duration is adequate for a hygiene phase
- Limitations: **n=26**, single centre; 6 weeks only; no randomisation of which side gets which device
  described in the abstract (以原文为准); probing depth unchanged so the study cannot speak to
  attachment gain; patients were **untreated**, so results may not transfer to maintenance patients
  (contrast T83)
- Abstract does not report bleeding index values — 以原文为准

## Consistency with other evidence

- Consistent with T83 (1991 crossover, **treated** periodontal patients: proximal plaque **1.22** with
  IDB vs **1.71** with floss vs **2.32** brush-only) — same direction in a different population
- Consistent with T68's practical hierarchy in this project: interdental cleaning matters more than
  which tool, but where embrasures are open, brushes are mechanically superior
- Tension with T20 (Cochrane on floss): floss evidence for caries/gingivitis is weak; this pair
  suggests IDB is the stronger approximal tool where it fits
