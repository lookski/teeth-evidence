# T56. Brushing right after acidic food wears enamel most: wait at least 1 hour

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 03-daily-routine](../topics/03-daily-routine.md) · `T56`

## Bottom line

The popular advice "brush immediately after eating" is wrong in the acidic case.
In an in situ study, enamel slabs eroded by citric acid for 3 min lost **0.258 +/- 0.141 um** when
brushed immediately, 0.224 +/- 0.087 um after 30 min of saliva exposure, and 0.195 +/- 0.075 um
after 60 min; the 60-min group was significantly lower than the immediate group (**p<0.001**).
The authors advise people at risk of erosive tooth wear to **wait at least 1 h** after acidic
food or drink before brushing.

## Study designs and populations

- Design: in situ experiment (slabs carried in the mouth)
- Sample: 7 female subjects, mean age 22 years
- Substrate: buccal slabs of human premolars, immersed in citric acid 3 min to produce
  moderate erosive lesions
- Exposure arms: 0 min (brush immediately), 30 min, or 60 min in the oral milieu
- Brushing: 30 s with toothpaste using each volunteer's preferred technique
- Outcome: mean surface substance loss (um)

## Key numbers (verbatim from abstract)

| Brushing time after acid exposure | Surface loss (um, mean +/- SD) |
|---|---|
| **Immediately (0 min)** | **0.258 +/- 0.141** |
| After 30 min | 0.224 +/- 0.087 |
| After 60 min | 0.195 +/- 0.075 |

- 60-min vs 0-min: significantly lower abrasion (**p<0.001**); 30-min vs 60-min also differed (p<0.001)
- Multiple linear regression: abrasion associated with saliva exposure (p=0.026), severity of the
  erosive attack (p<0.001), and resting salivary secretion rate (p=0.029)

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- Monotonic gradient: the longer the wait, the less substance is lost (0.258 -> 0.224 -> 0.195 um)
- Magnitudes are sub-micron and come from a **single** brushing episode; repeated daily over decades
  is what accumulates into clinically visible wear
- Mechanism: acid softens the superficial enamel, saliva needs time to remineralise it; brushing
  before that finishes abrades the softened layer
- Resting salivary flow is an independent predictor (p=0.029), so **dry-mouth people (including
  medication-induced xerostomia, T39) carry higher risk** and should follow the wait strictly
- Practical: after cola, juice, wine or citrus, rinse with water and **wait at least 1 h** before
  brushing; brushing straight away is worse than waiting

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: in situ (real oral environment) with real human enamel; three time points compared
  directly; monotonic and statistically significant
- Limitations: **only 7 subjects**, all 22-year-old women; 3-min citric acid is likely a harsher
  erosive challenge than typical meals; single-episode surface loss cannot be extrapolated to
  decades of accumulation; brushing force was not standardised (self-selected technique)

## Consistency with other evidence

- Consistent with T27 (erosion: fluoride cannot stop pH<3 acid attack) — erosion is a mechanism
  distinct from caries, and fluoride does not fix acid softening
- Directly contradicts the social-media claim "brush right after every meal for the cleanest teeth";
  this is the single most practical correction in this round
- Belongs with T40 (over-vigorous brushing and recession) in the "the brushing act itself can harm" cluster
