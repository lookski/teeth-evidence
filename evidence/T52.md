# T51. Obesity and periodontitis: meta-consistent association, youngest pay most

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 00-total](../topics/00-total.md) · `T51`

## Bottom line

Obesity is a reproducible risk marker for periodontitis: the 2022 updated meta gives
overall OR 1.35 (1.05-1.75) with a striking age gradient — OR 2.21 (1.26-3.89) in the
18-34 group vs negligible in older strata — consistent with the earlier Obes Rev review
(33 studies). Fat tissue is inflammatory plumbing for gum disease, and the association
is strongest before confounding accumulates with age.

## Study designs and populations

- Design 1: updated systematic review + meta (observational studies; Newcastle-Ottawa
  risk-of-bias; subgroups by age 18-34/35-54/55+ and country) — Front Endocrinol 2022
- Design 2: foundational Obes Rev review (33 studies; RCTs, cohorts, case-controls,
  cross-sections; WHO-defined overweight/obesity) — 2011

## Key numbers (verbatim from abstracts)

| Item | Result |
|---|---|
| Updated meta: obesity → periodontal disease | OR 1.35 (95% CI 1.05-1.75) |
| Subgroup 18-34 years | OR 2.21 (1.26-3.89) |
| Subgroup by country | European countries highest |
| Obes Rev 2011 | 33 studies included; positive association overall |

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- Age paradox: the association is strongest in young adults — not because obesity is more
  damaging early, but because older cohorts' periodontitis is dominated by smoking,
  diabetes and cumulative inflammation that dilute the obesity signal
- Mechanism: adipose-derived TNF-α, IL-6, leptin and oxidative stress share pathways with
  periodontal destruction (Periodontol 2000 review, PMID 24320961)
- Practical: weight management belongs in periodontal risk-factor counseling, especially
  for patients under 35; periodontal inflammation markers improve with weight loss
  (cytokine meta, PMID 37798684)

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: two independent meta generations (2011/2022) agree; clean age subgroup signal
- Limitations: observational; residual confounding (diet quality, smoking, SES); BMI is a
  crude exposure (no visceral-fat measurement in most studies)

## Consistency with other evidence

- Fits the metabolic-inflammation cluster: T07 (diabetes bidirectionality), T51 (kidney),
  T35 (endothelial) — periodontitis tracks cardiometabolic risk end to end
- The 18-34 OR 2.21 mirrors T47's message: young adults' oral futures are decided early
