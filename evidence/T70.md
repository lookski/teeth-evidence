# T70. Whitening toothpaste matches ordinary paste over 12 weeks; whitening mouthrinses and 10% CP actually work

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 06-hot-topics](../topics/06-hot-topics.md) · `T70`

## Bottom line

120 bovine specimens were darkened in coffee, then treated for a simulated **12 weeks** (groups 1-5)
or **14 days** (group 6, 10% carbamide peroxide). One-way ANOVA showed significant ΔE differences
among groups (**p=0.001**). Tukey testing put **Listerine Whitening, Colgate Plax Whitening and 10%
carbamide peroxide (CP) significantly above** the conventional fluoride toothpaste, the whitening
toothpaste Close Up White Now, and the experimental Plasdone rinse. Conclusion: the **whitening
toothpaste was no better than ordinary toothpaste**, while **whitening mouthrinses matched 14 days
of 10% CP bleaching**.

## Study designs and populations

- Design: in vitro whitening efficacy comparison
- Specimens: **120 cylindrical bovine teeth** specimens, darkened **24 hours in coffee**
- Colour measurement: spectrophotometer, **CIE L*a*b*** system
- Six groups by agent:
  1. Conventional **fluoridated toothpaste**
  2. **Close Up White Now** (whitening toothpaste)
  3. **Listerine Whitening** (whitening mouthrinse)
  4. **Colgate Plax Whitening** (whitening mouthrinse)
  5. Experimental mouthrinse with **Plasdone**
  6. **10% carbamide peroxide** (Whiteness Perfect) — the positive control
- Treatment simulation: **12 weeks** for groups 1-5; **14 days** for group 6
- Analysis: one-way ANOVA (alpha=0.05) then Tukey test

## Key numbers (verbatim from abstract)

| Finding | Result |
|---|---|
| ΔE difference among groups after 12 weeks | significant, **p=0.001** |
| **Higher colour alteration** | groups **3 (Listerine), 4 (Colgate Plax), 6 (10% CP)** |
| **Lower colour alteration** | groups **1 (conventional paste), 2 (Close Up White Now), 5 (Plasdone)** |
| Whitening toothpaste vs conventional paste | **similar** (both in the low group) |
| Whitening mouthrinses vs 10% CP | **similar** to each other |
| Absolute ΔE values | **not reported in abstract — 以原文为准** |

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- The cleanest takeaway: **whitening toothpaste does essentially nothing beyond ordinary toothpaste**
  over a 12-week course — the product category's core claim fails this test
- The surprise: **whitening mouthrinses** produced colour change comparable to 14 days of 10%
  carbamide peroxide. If replicated, that is a genuine low-cost bleaching route
- Practical hierarchy for real whitening: **10% CP bleaching ≈ whitening mouthrinses > whitening
  toothpaste ≈ ordinary toothpaste**
- Caveat: bovine enamel is more porous than human enamel, so absolute ΔE overstates what happens in
  a human mouth; only the rank order is transferable
- And note the tension with T69: blue covarine gives instant *appearance*, while these rinses gave
  measurable ΔE — two different mechanisms being measured by the same metric

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: six-way comparison including a **positive control** (10% CP) and a conventional-paste
  negative control; uses the CIE L*a*b* spectrophotometric standard rather than a visual shade guide;
  long simulation (12 weeks) for the rinses and paste
- Limitations: **in vitro**, bovine substrate, coffee-only stain; abstract omits absolute ΔE values;
  the "12 weeks" for rinses is a simulation that may overstate real exposure; no enamel-safety or
  sensitivity endpoint; single brands per category
- The mouthrinse result deserves independent replication before being recommended

## Consistency with other evidence

- Consistent with T69: optical agents change appearance, not tooth colour — different from the ΔE
  changes seen here for rinses and CP
- Consistent with T66 (charcoal, covarine, peroxide, microbeads head-to-head): real bleaching agents
  (peroxide) and specific technologies outperform plain pastes
- Consistent with T67/T68's disagreement: the "whitening toothpaste" category is heterogeneous and
  most products deliver little
- Cross-link T20/T68 for the point that mechanical plaque control, not whitening, is the health claim
  that matters
