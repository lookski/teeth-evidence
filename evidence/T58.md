# T58. Oil pulling vs 0.2% chlorhexidine: equal plaque control, less staining

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 03-daily-routine](../topics/03-daily-routine.md) · `T58`

## Bottom line

In an observer-masked randomized crossover trial (29 volunteers, 4-day plaque regrowth model),
coconut-oil pulling produced plaque inhibition **similar to 0.2% chlorhexidine** — plaque index
1.67 +/- 0.24 vs 1.61 +/- 0.20 — with **less staining** (stain index 0.21 +/- 0.13 vs 0.47 +/- 0.27).
Gingival index and bleeding on probing were similar between groups (p>0.05). This is the strongest
independent result in the oil-pulling literature: not a cure, but a genuine antiplaque mouth rinse
equivalent with a better side-effect profile.

## Study designs and populations

- Design: observer-masked, randomized, crossover clinical trial
- Model: 4-day supragingival plaque regrowth (a standard short-term antiplaque screening model)
- Sample: 29 volunteers
- Comparators: coconut oil pulling vs 0.2% chlorhexidine gluconate (CHX) mouthrinse
- Washout: 14 days between periods; each rinse used for 4 days
- Outcomes on day 5: plaque index (PI), gingival index (GI), stain index (SI), bleeding on probing (BOP)

## Key numbers (verbatim from abstract)

| Outcome | Oil pulling | 0.2% CHX |
|---|---|---|
| Plaque index (PI) | **1.67 +/- 0.24** | 1.61 +/- 0.20 |
| Stain index (SI) | **0.21 +/- 0.13** | 0.47 +/- 0.27 |
| Gingival index (GI) | similar | similar (p>0.05) |
| Bleeding on probing (BOP) | similar | similar (p>0.05) |

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- The result to quote: **plaque control equivalent to chlorhexidine, staining roughly half**
- Important framing: this is a 4-day regrowth model, not a long-term caries or periodontitis trial.
  It shows antiplaque *activity*, not disease prevention
- Chlorhexidine's real clinical problem is staining and taste disturbance on long-term use; oil
  pulling's advantage is exactly there
- Practical: oil pulling can be offered as an adjunct for people who cannot tolerate CHX, but it
  does **not** replace mechanical cleaning (toothbrush + interdental, T20/T68) — no trial shows it
  removes established biofilm the way brushing does
- Do not overstate: "equal plaque index in 4 days" is not "reverses gingivitis" or "detoxifies"

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: randomized, observer-masked, crossover (each subject is their own control);
  head-to-head against an active positive control rather than water; multiple indices including
  the side-effect endpoint (staining)
- Limitations: **n=29, 4 days only**; short-term plaque regrowth does not predict long-term
  gingivitis/periodontitis outcomes; single oil type and single protocol; no blinding of participants
  (oil and CHX are perceptibly different); no caries endpoint
- This is still a small trial — treat the equivalence as "similar in this model"

## Consistency with other evidence

- Consistent with T59 (systematic review of 4 RCTs, 182 participants: significant reductions in
  salivary bacterial colony count p=0.03 and plaque index p<0.001)
- Consistent with T60 (30-day coconut-oil pulling cohort, plaque and gingival indices fell from day 7)
- The cluster as a whole supports "modest, real antiplaque effect" — but all three are small and
  short, and none has a hard clinical endpoint
