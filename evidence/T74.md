# T74. Mouthwash and oral cancer: a 30-year literature of at least 10 case-control studies

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 06-hot-topics](../topics/06-hot-topics.md) · `T74`

## Bottom line

A short update review states that the possible relationship between mouthwash use and oral cancer
risk "has been the subject of **at least 10 case-control studies** published over the last three
decades". That is the review's substantive content: the literature is **large, entirely
observational, and still inconclusive**. The abstract is only 583 characters and reports no pooled
estimate, no effect size and no conclusion statement — for the review's actual verdict,
以原文为准.

## Study designs and populations

- Design: narrative review / update
- Scope: case-control studies of mouthwash use and oral cancer risk
- Literature volume: **at least 10 case-control studies** over roughly three decades (through 2008)
- Abstract length: ~583 characters — the shortest in this round

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- The honest read of this entry: it documents **how much** observational evidence exists, not what
  it shows. Use it as a bibliographic anchor, not as an effect estimate
- With >=10 case-control studies and no consensus, the field's problem is the same one that plagues
  all mouthwash-cancer work: **confounding by indication and behaviour** is severe (people who use
  mouthwash heavily differ in smoking, alcohol, dental care and oral health)
- Practical: T72 and T73 give the concrete numbers; this review tells you the question has been asked
  repeatedly without resolution. Do not present a single case-control OR as settled science
- The absence of a pooled meta-analysis in this review is itself notable — the literature has not
  been quantitatively synthesised here

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: establishes the size and duration of the evidence base; a recognised author in oral
  cancer epidemiology (La Vecchia); useful as a citation to show the debate is decades old
- Limitations: **abstract carries no data** — no ORs, no CIs, no heterogeneity assessment, no pooled
  analysis; narrative rather than systematic; ends in 2008, so it predates the ADH7 interaction work
  in T73 being widely discussed; cannot be used to support or refute causation on its own
- Effectively an evidence-map entry, not an evidence-strength entry

## Consistency with other evidence

- Consistent in scope with T72 (1991) and T73 (2014) — the two concrete case-control datasets in
  this cluster; both point toward elevated risk with heavy use
- Consistent with T73's ADH7 effect modification being the strongest mechanistic fragment available
- Should be cited **alongside** T72 and T73, never alone, and always with the observational caveat
- Cross-link T75 for the better-controlled (randomised crossover) harm signal from antibacterial
  mouthwash on blood pressure — a different endpoint, but the cleanest causal design in the cluster
