# T81. Baking soda for stain removal and whitening: a review with no numbers in the abstract

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 06-hot-topics](../topics/06-hot-topics.md) · `T81`

## Bottom line

A JADA review of baking-soda dentifrice for stain removal and whitening frames the field around
**two mechanisms**: **chemical** (peroxides for bleaching) and **mechanical** (abrasives in
prophylactic pastes and dentifrices to remove stains). Baking soda works through the mechanical route.
The abstract states the framework but reports **no effect sizes, no trial counts, and no comparative
results** — every quantitative claim about baking soda whitening is 以原文为准.

## Study designs and populations

- Design: **review of literature** (narrative)
- Topic: stain removal and whitening by baking soda dentifrice
- Framework presented: two mechanisms of stain removal —
  - **chemical**: peroxides for tooth bleaching
  - **mechanical**: abrasives in prophylactic pastes and dentifrices
- Quantitative synthesis: **none reported in abstract**

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- The useful contribution is the **mechanism taxonomy**, which matches the structure used across
  T66-T71 in this project: chemical bleaching (peroxide), mechanical removal (abrasives), and optical
  effects (blue covarine, T69) are three different things that marketing lumps together as "whitening"
- Placing baking soda in the **mechanical** bucket is correct: it is a mild abrasive that helps lift
  surface stain; it does not bleach
- Practical: baking soda paste can help with extrinsic stain (coffee, tea) through abrasion, but it
  will not change intrinsic tooth colour. For that you need peroxide (T70)
- Do not quote whitening percentages from this entry — none exist in the abstract

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: clear mechanistic framework; published in JADA, a clinically-oriented journal with wide
  readership; correctly separates bleaching from abrasive stain removal
- Limitations: **narrative review**, single author; abstract carries **no data** — no effect sizes,
  no study count, no quality assessment; cannot be used to quantify baking soda's whitening effect;
  potential conflict of interest not disclosed in the abstract (baking soda reviews are often
  industry-adjacent)
- Evidence-map entry: useful for framing, useless for effect estimation

## Consistency with other evidence

- Consistent with T79 (baking soda: significant plaque benefit vs placebo, small effect sizes
  -0.08 to -0.20) — a modest mechanical/chemical adjunct
- Consistent with T80 (in situ: no significant advantage over fluoride alone for caries protection)
- Consistent with T66-T71's taxonomy: mechanical (abrasive) < chemical (peroxide) for real colour change
- The cluster verdict on baking soda: **mild stain-removal and plaque adjunct, not a whitening
  breakthrough, not a fluoride replacement**
