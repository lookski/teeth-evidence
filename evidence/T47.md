# T47. Orthodontic white-spot lesions: the price of fixed appliances (and how to pay less)

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 00-total](../topics/00-total.md) · `T47`

## Bottom line

Fixed-appliance orthodontics carries a heavy, predictable caries cost: new white-spot
lesions (WSLs) develop in ~46% of patients within 12 months (meta: incidence 45.8%,
prevalence 68.4% across 14 studies); lesions concentrate in the first 6 months and can
persist cosmetically ≥5 years after debonding — prevention must start at bracket-on.

## Study designs and populations

- Design 1: meta-analysis of 14 studies on WSL incidence/prevalence during fixed treatment
- Design 2: longitudinal cohorts at 6/12 months vs bonded controls (n≈150 per study)
- Design 3: 5-year post-treatment prevalence study (51 treated vs 47 untreated 19-year-olds)

## Key numbers (verbatim from abstracts)

| Item | Result |
|---|---|
| WSL incidence during treatment (meta, 14 studies) | 45.8% |
| WSL prevalence during treatment | 68.4% |
| Longitudinal: ≥1 WSL at 6 months | 38% (vs 11% bonded control) |
| ≥1 WSL at 12 months | 46% |
| Early concentration | Sharp rise in first 6 months, slower rise to 12 |
| Sex skew among affected | 76% male vs 24% female (P = .009) |
| 5 years post-debonding | Orthodontic group still had higher WSL scores (esthetic concern) |

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- Timing: risk is front-loaded — the first 6 months of treatment drive most new lesions;
  hygiene checks and intensified prevention must begin at bracket placement, not mid-treatment
- Practical for patients/parents: electric toothbrush + interdental brushes + fluoride
  (toothpaste ≥1,500 ppm; varnish 2×/year per T46; high-fluoride paste 5,000 ppm for
  high-risk, see T49); diet control (T19/T27)
- Practical for clinicians: schedule oral-hygiene reviews at 6 weeks and 6 months;
  consider fluoride-releasing bonding materials; document baseline enamel

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: meta + prospective cohorts with controls; consistent time course
- Limitations: mostly single-center convenience samples; visual scoring subjectivity;
  older trials predate high-fluoride standard care (likely overestimates today's risk)

## Consistency with other evidence

- Mechanistically identical to T19/T27 (sugar + plaque retention around brackets)
- Countermeasure evidence: T46 (varnish) shows protection in orthodontic populations
