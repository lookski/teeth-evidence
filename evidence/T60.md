# T60. 30-day coconut-oil pulling in adolescents: plaque and gingival indices fall from day 7

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 03-daily-routine](../topics/03-daily-routine.md) · `T60`

## Bottom line

In a 30-day study of **60 age-matched adolescents (16-18 y) with plaque-induced gingivitis**, adding
coconut-oil pulling to their oral hygiene routine produced a statistically significant decrease in
plaque and gingival indices **starting from day 7**, with scores continuing to fall through day 30.

以原文为准. Note this is a single-arm preliminary report, so regression to the mean and
co-intervention (they kept brushing) cannot be separated from a genuine pulling effect.

## Study designs and populations

- Design: preliminary report; single-group, before-after (no inert control arm described in abstract)
- Sample: **60** age-matched adolescent boys and girls, **16-18 years**
- Condition: plaque-induced gingivitis
- Intervention: coconut-oil pulling **added to** their existing oral hygiene routine
- Duration: **30 days**
- Assessment points: baseline, days 1, 7, 15 and 30 (plaque index and gingival index)
- Rationale given: coconut oil is predominantly medium-chain fatty acids, **45-50% lauric acid**

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- The usable signal is the **time course**: improvement detectable by day 7 and still improving at
  day 30 — consistent with a cumulative antiplaque mechanism rather than an instant effect
- What cannot be claimed from this design: that pulling *caused* the improvement. Without a
  no-pulling control, daily brushing plus attention effects explain part of the drop
- Practical: pulling is low-risk and may add a small antiplaque benefit on top of brushing; it is
  not a treatment for established gingivitis on its own
- Adolescent population (16-18 y) limits extrapolation to adults with periodontitis

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: reasonable sample for a preliminary report (60); repeated measures at 4 time points
  (1, 7, 15, 30 days) show a trajectory, not a single snapshot; both plaque and gingival indices
- Limitations: **no control group** (single-arm); preliminary report, likely not powered or
  pre-registered; no blinding described; indices are surrogates, no attachment or bleeding endpoint;
  abstract omits all numeric values; co-intervention with usual brushing is uncontrolled
- Read alongside T58 (randomized crossover vs CHX) for the controlled version of this claim

## Consistency with other evidence

- Direction consistent with T58 (plaque inhibition equal to 0.2% CHX, less staining) and T59
  (systematic review: plaque index p<0.001 across 4 RCTs)
- The cluster's weakness is uniform: all short-term, surrogate endpoints, small samples
- Contrast with T18 (30-year plaque-control programme) — the only evidence in this project showing
  decades-long disease prevention, and it is mechanical, not oil-based
