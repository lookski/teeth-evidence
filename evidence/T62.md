# T62. Water fluoridation (BMJ 2000 systematic review): 214 studies, benefit and fluorosis side by side

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 06-hot-topics](../topics/06-hot-topics.md) · `T62`

## Bottom line

A systematic review searching 25 electronic databases plus the web included **214 studies**. The
median increase in the proportion of children without caries was **14.6%** (range -5.0% to 64%), and
the median reduction in decayed/missing/filled teeth was **2.25 teeth** (range 0.5 to 4.4). At a
fluoride level of **1 ppm**, an estimated **12.5% (95% CI 7.0%-21.5%)** of exposed people would have
fluorosis they would find aesthetically concerning. This is the review that first put benefit and
harm in the same table with the same rigour.

## Study designs and populations

- Design: systematic review of studies on water fluoridation
- Search: **25 electronic databases** plus the world wide web
- Included: **214 studies**
- Outcomes: proportion of children without caries; decayed, missing and filled teeth (primary and
  permanent); dental fluorosis
- Exposure of interest: community water fluoride level, with 1 ppm modelled for the fluorosis estimate

## Key numbers (verbatim from abstract)

| Outcome | Value |
|---|---|
| Studies included | **214** |
| Increase in children without caries | range **-5.0% to 64%**, median **14.6%** |
| Reduction in decayed/missing/filled teeth | range **0.5 to 4.4**, median **2.25 teeth** |
| **Aesthetically concerning fluorosis at 1 ppm** | **12.5% (95% CI 7.0%-21.5%)** |

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- The ranges matter as much as the medians: the caries-free benefit ranged from **-5.0%** (i.e. no
  benefit or slightly worse) to **64%**, showing the effect is highly site-dependent
- The 1 ppm fluorosis estimate (12.5%) is the number to use when discussing cosmetic harm; it sits
  close to Cochrane 2015's 12% at 0.7 ppm (T61), i.e. the aesthetic fluorosis rate is stable across
  the two reviews
- Practical: fluoridation's benefit is population-level and dose-site dependent; the cosmetic cost
  is measurable and roughly one in eight at 1 ppm
- Do not quote the 64% upper bound as typical — it is the top of a wide range

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: very broad search (25 databases + web); large study base (214); reports the full range
  rather than only the median; quantifies the cosmetic harm
- Limitations: the abstract does not report bias-risk assessment or study design breakdown, so the
  quality distribution is 以原文为准; predates the modern bias tools used in T61; observational/
  ecological designs cannot control individual-level confounding
- Historically important as the companion to Cochrane 2015: two independent teams, same direction,
  same fluorosis order of magnitude

## Consistency with other evidence

- Consistent with T61 (Cochrane 2015: 35%/26% dmft/DMFT reductions; ~12% aesthetic fluorosis at 0.7 ppm)
- Consistent with T15 (fluoride toothpaste is the best-evidenced delivery route)
- The fluorosis numbers here and in T61 are the honest counterweight to pro-fluoridation advocacy;
  the caries numbers are the honest counterweight to anti-fluoridation advocacy — cite both
