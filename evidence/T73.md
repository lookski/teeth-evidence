# T73. ARCAGE: mouthwash 3+ times/day linked to OR 3.23 for upper aerodigestive cancer

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 06-hot-topics](../topics/06-hot-topics.md) · `T73`

## Bottom line

The European ARCAGE study compared **1963 patients** with incident cancer of the oral cavity,
oropharynx, hypopharynx, larynx or oesophagus against **1993 controls**. After full adjustment, the
OR for **frequent mouthwash use (3 or more times/day)** was **3.23 (95% CI 1.68-6.19)** for all
upper-aerodigestive-tract sites combined. Critically, the **rare ADH7 variant** (fast ethanol
metabolism) was **lower in mouthwash users (OR 0.53, 95% CI 0.35-0.81) than in never-users (0.97,
0.73-1.29)**, indicating effect modification (p-heterogeneity 0.065) — a mechanistic signal that
alcohol in mouthwash matters.

## Study designs and populations

- Design: multicentre case-control study (ARCAGE, Europe)
- Cases: **1963** patients with incident cancer of the oral cavity, oropharynx, hypopharynx, larynx
  or oesophagus
- Controls: **1993** controls
- Analysis: multiple logistic regression with **multiple confounders** adjusted; adjusted ORs with
  95% CIs
- Exposures: dental care (DC) score, oral hygiene (OH) score, **mouthwash frequency**, and genotypes
  **ADH7, ADH1B, ADH1C, ALDH2**
- Outcome: upper aerodigestive tract (UADT) cancer, all sites combined

## Key numbers (verbatim from abstract)

| Comparison | Adjusted OR (95% CI) |
|---|---|
| **Low vs high dental care score** | **2.36 (1.51-3.67)** |
| **Low vs high oral hygiene score** | **2.22 (1.45-3.41)** |
| **Mouthwash >=3 times/day** | **3.23 (1.68-6.19)** |
| ADH7 rare variant, mouthwash users | **0.53 (0.35-0.81)** |
| ADH7 rare variant, never-users | 0.97 (0.73-1.29) |
| Effect modification (p-heterogeneity) | **0.065** |
| ADH1B, ADH1C, ALDH2 variants | no relevant differences between users and non-users |

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- There **is** a frequency dimension: the elevated OR applies to **3+ times/day**, not casual use —
  this is the dose-response that makes the association harder to dismiss as pure confounding
- The ADH7 finding is the most interesting part: people who metabolise ethanol fast show a *lower*
  OR when they use mouthwash, which is the opposite of what a purely confounded association would
  predict. It points to **acetaldehyde from mouthwash alcohol** as a biologically active exposure
- Oral hygiene and dental care scores also carried big ORs (2.22-2.36), which is a reminder that
  poor oral health itself is a cancer risk marker — mouthwash use is embedded in that behavioural web
- Practical: if you use mouthwash, prefer **alcohol-free** formulations and don't use them 3+ times
  daily for years. That is a cheap, low-regret change

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: large multicentre case-control (1963/1993); full multivariable adjustment including
  tobacco, alcohol, education and oral health; **gene-environment interaction analysis** that gives a
  mechanistic handle; dose threshold specified (>=3/day)
- Limitations: **observational** — reverse causation is conceivable (people with early symptoms may
  use more mouthwash); recall bias on lifetime mouthwash frequency; the ADH7 interaction is
  borderline (**p-heterogeneity 0.065**) and based on small genotype subgroups; no data on alcohol
  content of specific products; residual confounding by oral hygiene remains
- Do not cite the OR 3.23 without also citing that it applies to heavy use and is observational

## Consistency with other evidence

- Consistent with T72 (1991 US case-control: 40% excess in men, 60% in women with regular use) —
  same direction, different continent, two decades apart
- Consistent with T74 (review: at least 10 case-control studies over three decades)
- Mechanistically coherent with the acetaldehyde pathway, and the ADH7 signal is the strongest
  supporting fragment in this cluster
- Independent of T75 (antibacterial mouthwash raises blood pressure 2.3 mmHg) — that finding comes
  from a randomised crossover and is not confounded, though it concerns a different mechanism
  (nitric oxide depletion, not carcinogenesis)
