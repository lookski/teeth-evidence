# T50. Periodontitis and chronic kidney disease: mortality signal in CKD 3-5

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 00-total](../topics/00-total.md) · `T50`

## Bottom line

Periodontitis loads extra mortality onto chronic kidney disease: in NHANES III linked
mortality (median 14.3-year follow-up), 10-year all-cause mortality in CKD stage 3-5 rose
from 32% to 41% with periodontitis — on par with diabetes' effect (43%) — and meta
supports severe-periodontitis OR 2.39 (1.70-3.36) for CKD association.

## Study designs and populations

- Design 1: NHANES III (n = 13,784; 861 with CKD) linked to mortality follow-up; Cox models
  comparing periodontitis vs traditional CKD mortality risk factors — JCP 2016
- Design 2: systematic review + meta (37 studies; 17 in meta; severe PD OR; incident
  rate ratio in cohorts) — Oral Dis 2019
- Design 3: diabetic-kidney cohort (Gila River community, type 2 diabetes, up to 22-year
  follow-up; macroalbuminuria/ESRD endpoints) — Diabetes Care 2007

## Key numbers (verbatim from abstracts)

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| CKD 3-5: 10-y all-cause mortality without periodontitis | 32% (95% CI 29-35%) |
| Same, with periodontitis | 41% (36-47%) |
| Benchmark: with diabetes (in CKD) | 43% (38-49%) |
| Meta: CKD-periodontitis, severe PD | OR 2.39 (1.70-3.36) |
| Meta: adjusted / precise criteria | OR 2.26 (1.69-3.01) for severe PD |
| Cohort incident rate ratio | IRR 1.73 |
| T2D → macroalbuminuria (vs none/mild PD) | Moderate PD 2.0×, severe 2.1×, edentulous 2.6× (P = 0.01) |
| T2D → ESRD | 2.3×, 3.5×, 4.9× respectively (P = 0.02) |

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- Gradient across periodontal severity in the diabetic-kidney cohort (none → moderate →
  severe → edentulous): the kidney endpoint tracks periodontal destruction
- Practical: CKD patients (and diabetics, T07) should receive periodontal care as standard;
  nephrology and dentistry referrals should be bidirectional
- Causality caution: association robust after adjustment, but no RCT yet shows periodontal
  therapy preserving kidney function (interventional meta inconclusive)

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: nationally representative mortality linkage; periodontitis benchmarked
  against diabetes as a mortality risk factor; severity gradient
- Limitations: observational; kidney function measured once at baseline; treatment-
  benefit studies underpowered

## Consistency with other evidence

- Extends T01 (all-cause mortality RR 1.46) to a high-baseline-risk subpopulation where
  absolute risk differences become large (9 percentage points over 10 years)
- Aligns with T07 (diabetic complications) — kidney is the shared end-organ
