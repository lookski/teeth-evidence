# T88. Oral microbiome biogeography: structured niches, and a direct interface with the immune system

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 06-hot-topics](../topics/06-hot-topics.md) · `T88`

## Bottom line

A Nature Reviews Microbiology review describes the human oral microbiota as "highly diverse and
[having] a complex ecology, comprising **bacteria, microeukaryotes, archaea and viruses**", with
"elaborate and highly structured **biogeography** that shapes metabolic exchange on a local scale".
It emphasises that the microbiota "**interfaces with the immune system** of the human host and has an
important role in not only the health of the oral cavity but also **systemic health**", and highlights
**candidate phyla radiation bacteria** and **non-bacterial members** as recent advances.

## Study designs and populations

- Design: **narrative review** (Nature Reviews Microbiology, 2024)
- Scope: diversity, biogeography and human health of the oral microbiome
- Taxonomic scope: **bacteria, microeukaryotes, archaea, viruses**
- Key concept: **biogeography** — distinct microbial communities at distinct oral microenvironments,
  with local metabolic exchange
- Recent advances highlighted: species-level biogeography of several oral niches; ecological role of
  **candidate phyla radiation (CPR) bacteria**; **non-bacterial members**
- Host interaction: **immune system interface**; relevance to oral and **systemic** disease

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- **Biogeography is the practically important idea**: the mouth is not one habitat. Tongue, buccal
  mucosa, tooth surface, gingival sulcus and saliva each host different communities. That is why
  "the oral microbiome" as a single test or single probiotic is conceptually shaky
- The **immune interface** point explains why dysbiosis matters beyond the mouth: the same biofilm
  that drives periodontitis also engages systemic inflammatory pathways (see T84's confounded
  cardiovascular signal, and T87's disease list)
- **CPR bacteria** are a genuinely new frontier — organisms that cannot yet be cultured, discovered
  by sequencing. Their clinical significance is not established
- Practical: nothing here changes daily advice. It explains why **site-specific mechanical cleaning**
  (T82/T83: interdental brushes for embrasures) matters more than any microbiome product

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: authoritative review venue; explicitly includes non-bacterial members (most reviews stop
  at bacteria); introduces biogeography as an organising principle rather than a list of species;
  current (2024)
- Limitations: **narrative review with no numbers in the abstract** — no effect sizes, no study counts;
  mechanistic and ecological framing rather than clinical outcomes; CPR bacteria's role is speculative;
  cannot support any intervention claim
- Use for conceptual framing only

## Consistency with other evidence

- Consistent with T87 (Microorganisms review: four kingdoms, systemic disease links) and T89
  (Periodontology 2000: key organisms, complex networks) — all three converge on ecological framing
- Consistent with T76 mechanically: the oral bacteria that reduce dietary nitrate to nitrite are part
  of this biogeography, and killing them has measurable systemic consequences (blood pressure +2.3 mmHg)
- Consistent with T82/T83 in practice: if communities differ by site, cleaning must be site-specific
  (interdental brushes in open embrasures, floss in tight contacts)
- Caution from T84: systemic associations from observational data are easily confounded
