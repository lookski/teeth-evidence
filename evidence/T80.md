# T80. In situ: fluoride + baking soda reduced demineralisation, but not significantly more than fluoride alone

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 06-hot-topics](../topics/06-hot-topics.md) · `T80`

## Bottom line

In a crossover in situ study over **three 28-day phases**, 10% sucrose was dripped onto sound enamel
blocks three times daily, followed 10 minutes later by a slurry of placebo, fluoride (F), or
fluoride-plus-baking-soda (F+NaHCO3) dentifrice. **F+NaHCO3 produced higher plaque fluoride than
placebo (p<0.05)** and **lower demineralisation / greater remineralisation than placebo (p<0.05)**,
**but the difference versus F dentifrice alone was not significant**. So baking soda adds no
measurable caries-protection beyond fluoride.

## Study designs and populations

- Design: **crossover in situ study**, **three phases of 28 days** each
- Substrate: enamel blocks (sound blocks challenged with sucrose; all blocks exposed to dentifrice slurry)
- Cariogenic challenge: **10% sucrose solution dripped three times a day**, only onto the sound blocks
- Intervention: after 10 minutes, a slurry of **placebo**, **fluoride (F)**, or **fluoride + baking
  soda (F+NaHCO3)** dentifrice dripped onto all enamel blocks
- Outcomes: **plaque fluoride concentration**, **enamel demineralisation**, **remineralisation**

## Key numbers (verbatim from abstract)

| Outcome | F+NaHCO3 vs placebo | F+NaHCO3 vs F alone |
|---|---|---|
| Plaque fluoride concentration | **higher, p<0.05** | **not significant** |
| Enamel demineralisation | **lower, p<0.05** | **not significant** |
| Enamel remineralisation | **greater, p<0.05** | **not significant** |
| Phases / duration | 3 phases x 28 days | — |

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- The result is a **null for the added ingredient**: baking soda did not improve on fluoride
- Mechanistically sensible: baking soda raises plaque pH and can increase plaque fluoride retention,
  which is why the F+NaHCO3 group beat placebo — but fluoride alone already achieves most of that
- Practical: baking soda toothpaste is a reasonable fluoride delivery vehicle with mild extra
  cleaning (T79); there is no caries-prevention case for choosing it **over** fluoride
- The sucrose challenge model is aggressive (3x/day pure sucrose) and the differences versus F alone
  might emerge only in higher-risk or longer scenarios — but this study does not show them

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: **in situ crossover** (real oral environment, each subject their own control);
  **three 28-day phases** is unusually long for in situ work; both plaque-fluoride and hard-tissue
  outcomes measured; placebo and active-fluoride comparators both present
- Limitations: small sample (abstract reports 10% sucrose protocol but not the number of volunteers —
  以原文为准); aggressive sucrose model may compress differences; abstract gives only significance,
  not effect sizes; single F concentration; no caries lesion outcome (only de/remineralisation
  surrogates)

## Consistency with other evidence

- Consistent with T79 (systematic review: baking soda beats placebo, but the advantage over positive
  controls does not hold in follow-up designs) — same pattern, different design
- Consistent with T15 (fluoride is the active that matters for caries)
- Cross-link T71/T79 for the stain-removal role, which is baking soda's more defensible benefit
