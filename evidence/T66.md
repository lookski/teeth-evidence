# T66. Whitening toothpaste head-to-head: charcoal is not the winner

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 06-hot-topics](../topics/06-hot-topics.md) · `T66`

## Bottom line

An in vitro study compared six whitening technologies in toothpaste — activated charcoal, blue
covarine, hydrogen peroxide, microbeads, and optimised abrasives — and found **statistically
significant differences between toothpastes (p<0.05)** in shade change. The conclusion names the
winners: **only blue covarine and microbeads showed whitening after the first use**; after simulated
continuous use the best was **blue covarine, then hydrogen peroxide, then microbeads**. **Activated
charcoal was not among the best performers.** The abstract gives rankings but no numeric shade-change
values — magnitudes are 以原文为准.

## Study designs and populations

- Design: in vitro whitening efficacy comparison
- Products by whitening technology: activated charcoal (B&W), blue covarine (WAD), hydrogen peroxide
  (LWA), microbeads (Oral B 3D White Perfection - 3DW), optimised abrasives (XW4D)
- Specimens randomly distributed into **6 groups**
- Colour measured with a **VITA-Classical scale** by a calibrated examiner at three timepoints:
  T0 (before first brushing), TI (after the first brushing cycle), TCU (after simulated continuous use)
- Analysis: shade difference ΔSGU between T0-TI and T0-TCU; Kruskal-Wallis and Dunn's test;
  Wilcoxon for cumulative effect; alpha = 0.05

## Key numbers (verbatim from abstract)

| Finding | Result |
|---|---|
| Between-toothpaste difference, TI and TCU | significant, **p<0.05** |
| Effect of time of use | significant, **p<0.05** |
| Whitening after **first use (TI)** | only **blue covarine (WAD)** and **microbeads (3DW)** |
| Best after **continuous use (TCU)** | **WAD**, then **LWA (peroxide)**, then **3DW** |
| Activated charcoal (B&W) | **not among the best performers** |
| Numeric ΔSGU values | **not reported in abstract — 以原文为准** |

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- The actionable conclusion: **charcoal does not outperform** blue covarine, peroxide or microbeads
- Mechanism split matters: blue covarine is an **optical** effect (deposits blue dye that cancels
  yellow, making teeth *look* whiter immediately, no stain removal); peroxide is a **chemical
  bleach**; microbeads/optimised abrasives are **mechanical** stain removal
- So "instant whitening" from a blue-covarine paste (see T68) is cosmetic light trickery, not
  removal of stain
- Practical: if buying a whitening toothpaste, choose one with a stated active (peroxide for real
  bleaching, blue covarine for immediate appearance); charcoal has no evidence advantage and its
  abrasivity is a separate concern (T67)

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: genuine head-to-head of 6 technologies in one model with the same colour scale and
  calibrated examiner; separates first-use from continuous-use performance; non-parametric tests
  appropriate for ordinal shade data
- Limitations: **in vitro**; the abstract reports only significance and rank order, **no effect
  sizes**; shade scale (VITA-Classical) is coarse; staining model and brushing simulation details
  are 以原文为准; no enamel-safety endpoint — faster whitening by abrasion is not automatically better
- Read together with T67 (charcoal abrasivity and the null result) and T69 (blue covarine's
  optical mechanism)

## Consistency with other evidence

- Consistent with T69 (blue covarine works immediately via optical effects) — corroborates the
  ranking here
- Consistent with T67 (charcoal pastes show no whitening advantage over regular toothpaste)
- Inconsistent with marketing claims that charcoal is a superior whitener; the only charcoal study
  showing a benefit is T68's 1%-charcoal paste vs *regular* toothpaste, which still lost to
  covarine and peroxide in this head-to-head
