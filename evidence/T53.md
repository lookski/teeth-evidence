# T52. Oral frailty and tongue pressure: the mouth as an early-warning organ for aging

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 00-total](../topics/00-total.md) · `T52`

## Bottom line

Japan's gerodontology society formalized "oral hypofunction" (≥3 of 7 abnormal oral-function
items) and ~60% of community elders meet it; deteriorated swallowing function carries
frailty ORs of 6.4 (pre-frail) and 10.2 (frail) — the mouth declines years before the
body does, and tongue-pressure/oral exercise programs are the emerging countermeasure.

## Study designs and populations

- Design 1: Japanese Society of Gerodontology position paper (978 community elders
  65-85 y; 7-item oral function battery: hygiene, dryness, occlusal force, tongue-lip
  motor, tongue pressure, mastication, swallowing) — Gerodontology 2018
- Design 2: community cohort linking oral hypofunction to frailty (multinomial logistic
  regression, Kihon Checklist) — Geriatr Gerontol Int 2020
- Design 3: tongue-pressure-specific frailty analysis — Clin Interv Aging 2019

## Key numbers (verbatim from abstracts)

| Item | Result |
|---|---|
| Oral hypofunction prevalence (community elders) | ~60% |
| Oral hypofunction definition | Abnormalities in ≥3 of 7 oral-function items |
| Deteriorated swallowing → pre-frail | OR 6.4 (95% CI 3.9-10.8) |
| Deteriorated swallowing → frail | OR 10.2 (5.4-19.1) |
| Oral hypofunction → pre-frail | OR 1.4 (1.1-2.0) |
| Oral hypofunction → frail | OR 2.1 (1.2-3.5) |
| Strongest single item | Swallowing function |

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- The oral battery works like a frailty ECG: declining occlusal force, tongue pressure and
  swallowing flag systemic decline before weight or gait do
- Practical for elders and families: annual oral-function screening (the 7 items are
  chairside-testable); oral stretching/keeboom (tongue-pressure) exercises, chewing-side
  training and denture fit review are low-cost countermeasures
- Links back: masticatory nutrition (T36), pneumonia prevention via oral care (T10),
  chewing-brain axis (T13) — oral frailty is the umbrella concept connecting them

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: standardized 7-item definition (now used across Japan's long-term-care
  system); large community samples; consistent OR direction
- Limitations: cross-sectional base (frailty and oral decline co-evolve); Japanese
  populations dominate — generalizability to other diets/health systems unknown;
  intervention trials still small

## Consistency with other evidence

- Unifies T10 (oral care → pneumonia mortality), T13 (chewing-brain perfusion),
  T36 (dentition → nutrient intake) under one clinical construct
- The swallowing OR 10.2 is among the largest oral-systemic associations in this handbook
