# T71. "Whitening" products: most remove little chemical stain, a few reach 100%

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 06-hot-topics](../topics/06-hot-topics.md) · `T71`

## Bottom line

Five in vitro studies tested **39 agents** — 28 whitening products, 7 experimental formulations,
2 oxidising mouthrinses as positive controls, 1 fluoride toothpaste as benchmark, and water as
negative control — against a saliva/chlorhexidine/tea stain (optical density >= 2.0). Only **3
toothpaste products reached 100% stain removal by 5 minutes** (2 of them in 3 of the 4 studies that
used them), plus 4 experimental formulations. The majority of agents had **low total chemical stain
removal and prolonged times to 75% stain remaining**.

## Study designs and populations

- Design: five separate in vitro studies
- Agents: **39 total** — 28 whitening products, 7 experimental formulations, 2 oxidising mouthrinses
  (positive controls), 1 popular fluoride toothpaste (benchmark), 1 water (negative control)
- Stain model: saliva / chlorhexidine / tea stain developed on optically clear acrylic to an optical
  density of **at least 2.0**
- Exposure: standard slurries or solutions, **1-minute periods up to 5 minutes**
- Measurement: optical density at every 1-minute time point
- Analyses: **per cent stain remaining after 5 minutes**; **time to 75% stain remaining**

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- The distribution is the finding: a **minority** of whitening products actually dissolve stain
  chemically; the majority barely do
- The two metrics used (per cent remaining at 5 min, time to 75% remaining) are a sensible pairing —
  a product can look good at 5 minutes yet be slow, so both matter
- Practical: "whitening" on a label is not a regulated efficacy claim; expect most products to
  underperform. Choose products with a disclosed active (peroxide, or a proven chelator) rather than
  the word "whitening"
- Chemical stain removal is a different pathway from abrasive removal and from optical effects —
  this study isolates the **chemical** route specifically

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: very broad product sampling (**39 agents**, 28 commercial whitening products); includes
  both positive and negative controls plus a benchmark; standardised artificial stain with a defined
  optical density; two complementary outcome metrics
- Limitations: **in vitro artificial stain** (saliva/chlorhexidine/tea on acrylic) — not natural
  tooth stain, which is harder to remove; only 5 minutes of exposure, so slow-acting agents are
  penalised; the abstract does not name the 3 successful toothpastes — 以原文为准
- Cannot say which active ingredients worked; only that a small subset of products did

## Consistency with other evidence

- Consistent with T70 (whitening toothpaste no better than ordinary paste over 12 weeks) and T67
  (charcoal no advantage) — the "whitening paste" category is broadly weak
- Consistent with T66 (only specific technologies — covarine, peroxide, microbeads — outperformed)
- Consistent with T69: instant appearance effects are not chemical stain removal
- The honest synthesis across T66-T71: **most whitening toothpastes do little; real whitening needs
  peroxide bleaching**
