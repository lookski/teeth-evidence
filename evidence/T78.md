# T78. Coconut oil narrative review: lauric acid behaves as both medium- and long-chain

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 06-hot-topics](../topics/06-hot-topics.md) · `T78`

## Bottom line

A narrative review of coconut oil's health effects notes that coconut oil is rich in **lauric acid
(C12:0)**, which behaves as **both a medium-chain and a long-chain fatty acid** — the biochemical
detail that oil-pulling advocates cite as the rationale for antimicrobial action. The abstract is
essentially that single mechanistic fragment; there are **no clinical efficacy results for oral
health** in the abstract. For any actual oral-health claim, 以原文为准.

## Study designs and populations

- Design: **narrative review** (not systematic, not meta-analytic)
- Topic: health effects of coconut oil
- Relevant content for this project: lauric acid (C12:0) as the principal fatty acid, and its
  dual medium-chain/long-chain behaviour
- Oral-health endpoints: **not reported in the abstract**

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- This entry contributes **mechanistic background only**: it explains why lauric acid is proposed as
  the antimicrobial component in coconut-oil pulling
- It provides **no** evidence that pulling works. Do not cite it as efficacy evidence
- The medium/long-chain duality matters because medium-chain fatty acids are the ones with reported
  antimicrobial activity, while long-chain behaviour affects how the oil is metabolised — an
  interesting pharmacological point, not a clinical finding
- Practical: use this only to explain the proposed mechanism; use T58/T59/T60/T77 for outcomes

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: a recognised review author; correctly states the lauric-acid chemistry that underpins
  the pulling hypothesis
- Limitations: **narrative review** — no systematic search, no quality assessment, no pooled
  estimates; abstract contains no oral-health data at all; cannot support any efficacy claim;
  commercial interest in coconut oil is widespread, though not disclosed in the abstract
- Weakest entry in the oil-pulling cluster by design; included only for completeness of the mechanism

## Consistency with other evidence

- Provides the mechanism assumed by T60 (which notes coconut oil is **45-50% lauric acid**)
- Does **not** corroborate T58 (plaque index equal to CHX), T59 (4 RCTs, plaque p<0.001) or T77
  (triple-blind RCT) — those supply the outcomes; this supplies the rationale
- The oil-pulling cluster's structure: mechanism (T78) + short-term RCTs (T58, T77) + systematic
  review (T59) + cohort (T60). Verdict: modest short-term antiplaque effect, no hard endpoints
