# T59. Oil pulling systematic review: 4 RCTs, 182 participants, real but tiny evidence base

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 03-daily-routine](../topics/03-daily-routine.md) · `T59`

## Bottom line

A systematic review of coconut-oil pulling found 42 eligible records but only **4 RCTs with 182
participants** met inclusion, each lasting **7 to 14 days**. Within that thin base, pulling
significantly reduced salivary bacterial colony count (**p = 0.03**) and plaque index score
(**p < 0.001**), and one trial showed significantly less staining than chlorhexidine (**p = 0.0002**).
Conclusion: the effect looks real in short antiplaque models, but the entire literature is four
small short trials — nowhere near enough to claim caries or gingivitis prevention.

## Study designs and populations

- Design: systematic review
- Search yield: 42 eligible studies screened
- Included: **4 RCTs, 182 participants total**
- Trial duration: 7 to 14 days (all short-term antiplaque/regrowth models)
- Outcome domains: salivary bacterial colony count, plaque index, staining (vs chlorhexidine)

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- The most informative number here is the evidence volume, not the p-values: **182 people, 1-2 weeks**
- All four trials used plaque/bacteria surrogates; none measured caries incidence, attachment loss,
  or gingivitis progression over months
- Practical: pulling is a plausible adjunct with a good side-effect profile (less staining than CHX),
  but it is **not** a substitute for mechanical plaque control (toothbrush + interdental cleaning)
- Anyone with active periodontitis should not swap proven care for oil pulling on the strength of
  4 short RCTs

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: systematic search; restricts to RCTs only (the right design filter); reports both
  benefit (plaque, bacteria) and comparative side-effect (staining)
- Limitations: **only 4 RCTs / 182 participants**, all 7-14 days; abstracts of the included trials
  report surrogates; heterogeneity in oils, protocols and indices not addressed in the abstract;
  publication bias risk is high in a small complementary-therapy literature
- Overlaps T58 (the crossover trial against 0.2% CHX) — that trial is likely one of the four included

## Consistency with other evidence

- Consistent with T58 (plaque inhibition similar to 0.2% CHX, less staining) and T60 (30-day
  cohort showing plaque/gingival index falls from day 7)
- The three together say: modest, reproducible short-term antiplaque effect; zero evidence for
  hard clinical endpoints
- Contrast with T20 (Cochrane on floss) and T68 (interdental brushes): mechanical methods at least
  have long-term outcome data, which pulling does not
