# T84. Interdental cleaning linked to HR 0.2 for new cardiovascular events in CHD patients

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 06-hot-topics](../topics/06-hot-topics.md) · `T84`

## Bottom line

In **942 inpatients with coronary heart disease (CHD)** followed for **1 year**, patients who reported
**practising interdental cleaning** (floss/interdental brushes) had a **significantly decreased
adjusted risk for new cardiovascular events: HR = 0.2 (95% CI 0.06-0.6, p = 0.01)** compared with
those who did not. The primary endpoint occurred in **7.3%** of patients (MI 2.1%, stroke/TIA 1.8%,
cardiovascular deaths 3.4%; n = 941 completed follow-up). Severe periodontitis (HR 1.2, CI 0.7-2.1,
p = 0.53) and IL-6 genotypes were **not** significantly associated.

## Study designs and populations

- Design: prospective cohort with **1-year follow-up**
- Sample: **942 inpatients with CHD**; **941** completed the 1-year follow-up
- Exposures assessed: periodontitis status, **oral care habits (including interdental cleaning aids)**,
  subgingival biofilm bacteria, **IL-6 -174 genotypes (rs1800793)**
- Outcomes: new cardiovascular events within 1 year
- Adjustment (Cox regression): **age, gender, smoking, BMI, use of interdental hygiene aids, plaque
  index, severe periodontitis, diabetes, hypertension, dyslipoproteinaemia, missing teeth, serological
  parameters, IL-6 genotypes**
- Primary endpoint components: myocardial infarction, stroke/TIA, cardiovascular death

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- **HR 0.2 is implausibly large** for a hygiene behaviour and should not be read as "flossing cuts
  heart attacks by 80%". A 5-fold risk reduction from interdental cleaning alone signals residual
  confounding, not a miracle
- The authors themselves note that interdental cleaners were **younger, less likely male, less likely
  to have severe periodontitis, less tobacco exposure, fewer missing teeth, lower plaque and bleeding
  scores** — i.e. the exposure group is healthier on every measured axis. Adjustment helps but cannot
  fully remove that
- Notably, the "biological" variables (severe periodontitis, IL-6 genotype, oral species count) were
  **null** — which argues against a strong causal periodontal-cardiovascular pathway in this cohort
- Practical: interdental cleaning is good for gums (T82/T83) and that is a sufficient reason to do it;
  the cardiovascular claim needs a randomised trial that does not exist

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: prospective design with a defined 1-year window; hard clinical endpoint (MI, stroke, CV
  death), not a surrogate; extensive multivariable adjustment including serology and genotype;
  reports null findings for periodontitis and IL-6 rather than hiding them
- Limitations: **observational cohort** — healthy-adherer bias is severe (interdental cleaners differ
  on age, sex, smoking, disease severity); **HR 0.2 with CI 0.06-0.6 is too extreme to be causal**;
  self-reported oral care habits; 1-year follow-up is short for CV events; single-centre inpatient
  sample (hospitalised CHD patients, not stable outpatients); **n=942** with 7.3% events = ~69 events,
  a small number for a multivariable model with this many covariates (overfitting risk)
- This is the weakest causal claim in the interdental cluster and must be presented as hypothesis-generating

## Consistency with other evidence

- Consistent **mechanistically** with the periodontal-systemic inflammation literature (this project's
  sex/lifespan repos cover the CVD links from other angles)
- Consistent with T82/T83 on the dental side: interdental brushes are the better approximal tool
- **Inconsistent** with the internal null findings here: severe periodontitis showed no association
  (HR 1.2, p = 0.53), which undercuts a causal story where cleaning helps *because* it reduces
  periodontal inflammation
- The honest synthesis: interdental cleaning is well-evidenced for plaque and gingivitis; the
  cardiovascular benefit is an unproven, confounded association
