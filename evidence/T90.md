# T90. Bioactive glass in dentistry: a materials review, no clinical outcomes

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 06-hot-topics](../topics/06-hot-topics.md) · `T90`

## Bottom line

A 2019 review describes **bioactive glass (BAG)** as a biomaterial with "bioactive properties" that
make it "applicable to several clinical applications involving the **regeneration of hard tissues** in
medicine and dentistry". Dental uses listed: **restorative materials, mineralising agents, implant
coating, pulp capping, root canal treatment, and air-abrasion**. The abstract is a scope statement
with **no clinical outcome data** — for any efficacy claim, 以原文为准.

## Study designs and populations

- Design: **narrative review** of a biomaterial class
- Material: **bioactive glass (BAG)**
- Mechanism framing: activating "body immune cells or different proteins" so that "natural ground
  substance, tissue component and long-lasting tissues grow"
- Dental applications listed:
  - dental **restorative materials**
  - **mineralising agents**
  - coating material for **dental implants**
  - **pulp capping**
  - **root canal treatment**
  - **air-abrasion**
- Medical applications: orthopaedics to soft-tissue restoration

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- **Relevance to daily oral care is limited.** Bioactive glass appears in some **sensitivity**
  toothpastes as a "mineralising agent", which is the consumer-facing use; this review provides the
  materials-science context, not efficacy evidence
- For the sensitivity claim specifically, the evidence base is separate and not covered here —
  the honest position is that this entry documents **what the material is used for**, not whether
  those uses work
- Practical: if you see "bioactive glass" on a toothpaste, it refers to this class of calcium-sodium-
  phosphosilicate particles that can deposit mineral; treat marketing claims as unverified pending
  the relevant clinical trials
- Do not present this as support for any remineralisation or sensitivity benefit

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: broad coverage of dental applications; situates BAG within the wider biomaterials field;
  useful for understanding what the ingredient actually is
- Limitations: **narrative review with no outcome data in the abstract**; no trial counts, no effect
  sizes, no comparison against alternatives (e.g. fluoride, CPP-ACP); mechanism description is
  promotional in tone ("natural ground substance... long-lasting tissues grow"); single-topic scope
- Weakest entry in this round for clinical decision-making; included because bioactive glass appears
  in consumer toothpaste marketing

## Consistency with other evidence

- No direct counterpart in this project; it sits outside the caries/erosion/periodontal evidence
  streams (T15, T27, T56, T82/T83)
- Distinguish clearly: **fluoride** has the caries evidence (T15); **bioactive glass** has materials
  evidence for restorative and endodontic uses, and unproven consumer claims
- The project's standing caution applies: a named ingredient with a plausible mechanism is not the
  same as a demonstrated benefit (compare T67/T68 on charcoal, T79/T80 on baking soda)
