# T45. Pit-and-fissure sealants: Cochrane-grade caries prevention for molars

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 00-total](../topics/00-total.md) · `T45`

## Bottom line

Resin-based sealants on permanent molars cut caries odds by ~88% at 24 months in children
(OR 0.12, 95% CI 0.08-0.19, moderate-quality evidence, Cochrane 38 trials n=7,924) — with
fluoride, this is the most effective procedural prevention in dentistry, and it is
dose-timed: applied soon after molar eruption.

## Study designs and populations

- Design 1: Cochrane systematic review (38 RCTs, 7,924 children aged 5-16; GRADE-rated)
- Design 2: ADA/AAPD evidence-based clinical practice guideline (2008 + 2016 updates)

## Key numbers (verbatim from abstracts)

| Comparison | Result |
|---|---|
| Resin sealant vs no sealant, first permanent molars, 24 mo | OR 0.12 (0.08-0.19), 7 trials, 1,322 children evaluated |
| Translated: 16% baseline caries risk → sealed | 5.2% carious surfaces (95% CI 3.13-7.37%) |
| Translated: 40% baseline risk → sealed | 6.25% (3.84-9.63%) |
| Evidence quality | Moderate (GRADE) for resin vs none |

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- The absolute benefit scales with baseline risk: high-caries-risk children gain the most,
  but even average-risk molars benefit (160→52 per 1,000 at 24 months)
- Practical: seal within ~1-2 years of molar eruption (6- and 12-year molars); re-check
  integrity at recall visits; sealants complement — not replace — fluoride toothpaste
- Combined with T15 (fluoride) and T41 (xylitol), this completes the caries-prevention stack

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: Cochrane + GRADE; direct clinical endpoint (caries incidence)
- Limitations: early trials (1970s) predate current fluoride exposure; retention rates vary
  by operator skill; glass-ionomer sealants showed weaker protection than resins

## Consistency with other evidence

- Aligns with T15 (fluoride efficacy) — mechanical barrier + chemical remineralization stack
- ADA guideline (PMID 18310730) mirrors Cochrane conclusions for clinical practice
