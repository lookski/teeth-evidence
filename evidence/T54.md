# T53. Dental X-rays in 2026: doses are tiny, the 1980s fears are obsolete

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 00-total](../topics/00-total.md) · `T53`

## Bottom line

Modern intraoral dental radiography delivers ~35 µSv for a full-mouth series and ~5 µSv
for four bitewings (2007 ICRP dosimetry) — organ doses fell 86-96% from the 1940s to the
2000s — but with dose-tracking rules of thumb: 4 bitewings ≈ 1-2 days of background
radiation; FMX ≈ 1-2 weeks. Refuse routine annual films without clinical indication, but
do not delay indicated imaging for fear.

## Study designs and populations

- Design 1: dosimetry re-analysis under 2007 ICRP recommendations (effective doses for
  FMX, bitewings, panoramic, CBCT modalities) — J Am Dent Assoc 2008
- Design 2: seven-decade (1940-2009) historical reconstruction of brain/eye/salivary/
  thyroid absorbed doses per single intraoral film — Health Phys 2020

## Key numbers (verbatim from abstracts)

| Examination | Effective dose (2007 ICRP) |
|---|---|
| Full-mouth radiographs (PSP/F-speed + rectangular collimation) | 34.9 µSv |
| Four-image posterior bitewings | ~5 µSv (order of magnitude, per abstract) |
| Historical dose trend 1940→2009 | Brain -86%, eye lens -96%, salivary -95%, thyroid -89% |
| Highest single-film organ dose | Salivary glands: 0.23 mGy (1940s) → 0.025 mGy (2000s) |

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- Context anchors: annual natural background ≈ 3,000 µSv; transcontinental flight ≈
  30-50 µSv — a 4-film bitewing check is ~1-2 days of living; CBCT is higher (tens to
  hundreds of µSv) and needs case justification
- Practical: keep bitewing intervals risk-based (low-risk adults 2-3 years, high-risk
  6-18 months per radiology guidelines); rectangular collimation + F-speed/E-speed
  sensors are the modern standard — if your clinic still uses round collimation, ask
- The salivary gland historically received the highest dose → the 2013 meningioma case-
  control (not re-verified here, see limitation) concerned mostly old-era exposures

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: dosimetry measured on phantoms with both ICRP eras; seven-decade trend shows
  technology trajectory, not speculation
- Limitations: µSv→risk conversion is linear-no-threshold assumption, contested at low
  doses; this entry does not re-verify the meningioma literature (abstract numbers above
  are dosimetry only); CBCT doses vary by field of view

## Consistency with other evidence

- Compatible with T43 (third-molar imaging decisions) and T26 (impacted-molar follow-up):
  imaging when indicated, not by calendar
- The trend direction matches radiation protection across medicine (ALARA)
