# T49. Root caries and 5,000 ppm fluoride toothpaste: the strongest fluoride effect size in this handbook

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 00-total](../topics/00-total.md) · `T49`

## Bottom line

For root caries — the caries of aging dentition and dry mouths — prescription-strength
5,000 ppm fluoride toothpaste roughly halves lesion activity: active root lesions 1.05 vs
2.55, arrested lesions 2.13 vs 0.61 (8-month nursing-home RCT, staff-brushed), and a
2-year adolescent RCT shows a prevented fraction of 40-42% on caries progression.

## Study designs and populations

- Design 1: 8-month randomized clinical trial, 176 elderly disabled nursing-home
  residents (Copenhagen, 6 homes; staff brushed twice daily; 125 completed); 5,000 vs
  1,450 ppm NaF; root-caries lesion activity assessed by texture/contour/location/colour —
  Gerodontology 2013
- Design 2: 2-year single-blind RCT, 279 caries-active adolescents (211 completed, 76%);
  unsupervised brushing with Duraphat 5,000 ppm vs 1,450 ppm; compliance subgroups A
  (excellent) / B (poor, 28%) — Caries Res 2010

## Key numbers (verbatim from abstracts)

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Baseline active lesions (5,000 vs 1,450 ppm) | 2.61 vs 2.67 (SD 1.7 vs 1.8), no difference (p > 0.16) |
| Follow-up active root lesions | 1.05 (SD 2.76) vs 2.55 (SD 1.91) |
| Follow-up arrested root lesions | 2.13 (SD 1.68) vs 0.61 (SD 1.76) |
| Adolescents: caries progression | Lower with 5,000 ppm (A: p < 0.01, B: p < 0.001) |
| Prevented fraction, progression | 40% |
| Prevented fraction, incidence (compliance B) | 42% (p < 0.05) |

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- The fluoride-concentration gradient continues past 1,500 ppm: Cochrane (T15) shows
  1,000+ ppm beats no-fluoride and 1,500 ppm beats 1,000; here 5,000 beats 1,450 with
  the largest effect sizes in the handbook — the response is log-dose-like, not saturating
- Who benefits most: exposed root surfaces (recession, T40), xerostomia (T39),
  nursing-home residents needing staff-assisted brushing, radiation-head-and-neck and
  Sjögren patients; healthy teens with low caries need not upgrade
- Practical: 5,000 ppm is prescription-only in most countries and not a replacement for
  hygiene — it is the escalation step when root lesions are active despite 1,500 ppm;
  staff/family-assisted brushing + high-F paste is the nursing-home package (T10's
  oral-care logic plus T49's paste)

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: two RCTs at opposite ages and dependency levels; staff-brushed design
  removes compliance noise in the elderly trial; hard lesion-activity endpoints
- Limitations: single-examiner calibration; 8 months is short for caries; adolescent
  trial single-blind with self-reported compliance; no harmful-event signal but
  ingestion risk in cognitively impaired users needs supervision

## Consistency with other evidence

- Direct extension of T15 (fluoride toothpaste dose gradient) into the root-caries niche;
  complements T46 (varnish in toddlers) — same agent class, opposite life stage
- Consistent with T39 (dry mouth → caries) and T51/T53 (elderly oral decline): root
  caries is the aging-mouth lesion
