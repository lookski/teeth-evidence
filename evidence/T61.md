# T61. Water fluoridation (Cochrane 2015): 35% dmft reduction, but 97% of studies at high risk of bias

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 06-hot-topics](../topics/06-hot-topics.md) · `T61`

## Bottom line

The Cochrane review of community water fluoridation included **155 studies** (107 with data for
quantitative synthesis) and found that starting fluoridation reduces **dmft by 1.81 teeth
(95% CI 1.31-2.31)** and **DMFT by 1.16 (0.72-1.61)** — a **35% reduction in dmft and 26% in DMFT**
versus control medians — plus more caries-free children (+15% deciduous, +14% permanent).
**But** the authors state that **over 97% of studies were at high risk of bias**, most (71%) predate
1975 and the widespread use of fluoride toothpaste, and at 0.7 ppm about **12% (95% CI 8%-17%)** of
participants had fluorosis of aesthetic concern. This is the single best summary of both sides.

## Study designs and populations

- Design: Cochrane systematic review of water fluoridation studies
- Included: **155 studies** met criteria; **107** provided data for quantitative synthesis
- Outcomes: caries severity (dmft/DMFT), caries-free proportions, dental fluorosis
- Search: Cochrane Oral Health Trials Register (to 19 Feb 2015), CENTRAL, MEDLINE (1946-2015),
  EMBASE (1980-2015), Proquest, Web of Science, ZETOC
- Historical context given: fluoridation began in the USA in 1945, practised in about 25 countries

## Key numbers (verbatim from abstract)

| Outcome | Effect (95% CI) | Studies / participants |
|---|---|---|
| **dmft reduction** | **1.81 (1.31-2.31)** = **35% reduction** | 9 studies, 44 268 participants (high risk of bias) |
| **DMFT reduction** | **1.16 (0.72-1.61)** = **26% reduction** | 10 studies, 78 764 participants (high risk of bias) |
| Caries-free children, deciduous | **+15% (11%-19%)** | 10 studies, 39 966 participants |
| Caries-free children, permanent | **+14% (5%-23%)** | 8 studies, 53 538 participants |
| **Fluorosis of aesthetic concern, 0.7 ppm** | **~12% (8%-17%)** | 40 studies, 59 630 participants |
| Fluorosis of any level, 0.7 ppm | **40% (35%-44%)** | 90 studies, 180 530 participants |

- Methodological caveats stated explicitly: **>97% of studies at high risk of bias**; substantial
  between-study variation; **71% of studies predate 1975**
- Insufficient information on: whether fluoridation reduces SES disparities; the effect of
  **stopping** fluoridation programmes
- **No** studies meeting criteria on effectiveness in **adults**

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- Benefit side: ~1.8 fewer decayed/missing/filled deciduous teeth and ~1.2 fewer permanent teeth;
  modest in absolute terms but meaningful at population level
- Harm side: at 0.7 ppm, ~12% have fluorosis a dentist would consider aesthetically concerning;
  40% have fluorosis of any severity detectable under controlled clinical conditions
- Critical nuance for the social-media fight: most evidence predates **fluoride toothpaste**, which
  is now near-universal. The marginal benefit of water fluoridation *on top of* fluoride toothpaste
  is therefore likely smaller today than the 26-35% headline
- Practical: where water is fluoridated, the caries benefit is real and the aesthetic fluorosis rate
  is the main documented cost; where it is not, fluoride toothpaste achieves much of the same effect
- Stopping fluoridation has **not** been reliably evaluated — so claims in either direction about
  "what happens when you stop" exceed the evidence

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: the largest and most rigorous synthesis available; separates caries benefit from
  fluorosis harm with numbers on both sides; explicit about bias risk rather than hiding it
- Limitations: **>97% high risk of bias**; evidence mostly pre-1975, before fluoride toothpaste;
  no adult data; cannot answer the stop-fluoridation question; ecological designs predominate,
  so individual-level confounding is not controlled
- This entry should be cited *together* with T62 (2000 BMJ systematic review) and T63/T64
  (fluoride neurotoxicity) — never alone

## Consistency with other evidence

- Consistent with T62 (BMJ 2000: median 14.6% more caries-free children, 2.25 fewer teeth affected,
  12.5% aesthetically concerning fluorosis at 1 ppm)
- Consistent with T15 (fluoride toothpaste: 96 trials — the delivery route with the best evidence)
- Tension with T63/T64 (prenatal fluoride and IQ): those are about **ingested** fluoride in pregnancy,
  a different exposure window and route from topical caries prevention; they do not overturn T61's
  caries benefit but do bear on the risk-benefit calculus at population level
