# T83. Three-way crossover: interdental brush 1.22, floss 1.71, brush alone 2.32 proximal plaque

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 06-hot-topics](../topics/06-hot-topics.md) · `T83`

## Bottom line

**30 previously treated periodontal patients** completed a **three-way crossover** study (1 month per
period) comparing toothbrush only, toothbrush + floss, and toothbrush + interdental brush. Proximal
plaque scores were **2.32** (brush only), **1.71** (brush + floss) and **1.22** (brush + interdental
brush), with **statistically significant differences between the three groups**. Buccal/lingual plaque
was 0.64 / 0.62 / 0.51, and gingival index 0.37 / 0.36 / 0.32. Interdental brushes were clearly the
best approximal tool in maintenance patients.

## Study designs and populations

- Design: **three-way crossover study**
- Sample: **30 previously treated periodontal patients** (maintenance patients — contrast T82's
  untreated population)
- Arms: (1) toothbrush only, (2) toothbrush + **floss**, (3) toothbrush + **interdental brush**
- Duration: each trial period **1 month**
- Outcomes after each period: **gingivitis (GI)**, **buccal/lingual plaque**, **proximal plaque**

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- Clean **monotonic ordering**: brush only (2.32) > floss (1.71) > ID brush (1.22). Each step adds
  real approximal plaque removal
- The gap between floss and ID brush (1.71 vs 1.22) is larger than between brush-only and floss
  (2.32 vs 1.71) — i.e. **the brush is the bigger upgrade** once you decide to clean interdentally
- Gingival index differences are small (0.37 -> 0.32); proximal plaque is where the effect lives
- Practical: in maintenance patients with open embrasures, interdental brushes outperform floss;
  floss remains appropriate for tight contacts. Both beat brushing alone
- Crossover design means each patient experienced all three, so the ordering is not confounded by
  patient-level hygiene skill

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: **three-way crossover** — the strongest within-patient comparison design for this
  question; three outcome domains (GI, buccal/lingual plaque, proximal plaque); **1 month per arm**
  is long enough for plaque to re-establish; **previously treated** patients (maintenance, the
  population where interdental cleaning is actually prescribed)
- Limitations: **n=30**, single centre; 1991 data with older brush designs; abstract reports no
  CIs or p-values per comparison, only "statistically significant differences"; no bleeding-on-probing
  endpoint; no randomisation sequence described; no washout period mentioned — 以原文为准
- Population is maintenance patients, so results may not transfer to untreated periodontitis (T82)

## Consistency with other evidence

- Consistent with T82 (1998 split-mouth, **untreated** periodontitis: IDB 2.15 vs DF 2.47 approximal
  plaque at 6 weeks) — same ordering, different population, seven years apart
- Two independent designs (crossover vs split-mouth) and two populations (treated vs untreated) agree:
  **interdental brushes remove more approximal plaque than floss**
- Consistent with T68's practical framing in this project: pick the tool that fits the embrasure;
  where a brush fits, it cleans better
- Tension with T20 (Cochrane floss review): floss has weak evidence for hard outcomes; IDB has
  consistently better surrogate data
