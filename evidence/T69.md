# T69. Blue covarine: teeth look whiter immediately, but nothing is removed

> Hierarchy: [← README](../README.md) · [Topic overview 06-hot-topics](../topics/06-hot-topics.md) · `T69`

## Bottom line

An analysis of covariance on a silica whitening toothpaste containing **blue covarine** showed it was
**significantly more effective than the negative control toothpaste (p<0.05)** and made teeth
measurably **less yellow, less red and overall whiter immediately after brushing**. The key
interpretation: this is an **optical** effect — a blue dye deposits on the surface and cancels the
yellow hue, so the tooth *appears* whiter without any stain being removed or enamel bleached.
It works instantly and washes off.

## Study designs and populations

- Design: whitening efficacy study analysed by **ANCOVA**
- Product: silica whitening toothpaste containing **blue covarine**
- Control: negative control toothpaste (no whitening active)
- Outcome: colour measurement in three dimensions — **yellowness, redness, and overall whiteness**
- Timing: measured **immediately after brushing**

## Key numbers (verbatim from abstract)

| Comparison | Result |
|---|---|
| Blue covarine paste vs negative control | **significantly more effective, p<0.05** |
| Direction of colour change | teeth measurably **less yellow, less red, overall whiter** |
| Timing | **immediately after brushing** |
| Effect magnitude | **not reported in abstract — 以原文为准** |

## Dose-response and practical interpretation

- The honest claim for blue covarine is "**instant appearance of whiteness**", not "whitening"
- Mechanism: blue light absorption/deposition shifts perceived hue; the underlying tooth colour is
  unchanged. It is closer to makeup than to bleaching
- Because it is a surface deposit, the effect disappears when the film washes off — no cumulative
  benefit, no stain removal
- Practical: useful before an event (photos, a meeting) when instant appearance matters; useless as
  a long-term whitening strategy. For actual stain removal you need peroxide bleaching (T70) or
  professional treatment
- This also explains why T66 ranked blue covarine highest "after first use" — the study measured
  appearance, and covarine wins appearance contests by design

## Strengths and limitations

- Strengths: uses a proper negative control; measures three colour axes rather than a single
  whiteness score; ANCOVA controls for baseline shade; immediate-effect timing matches the product's
  actual mechanism
- Limitations: **industry-authored** (the paste is a commercial blue-covarine product); short
  immediate readout only, no durability data in the abstract; effect sizes not reported; no
  comparison against peroxide bleaching, so "how much of a real bleach is this" is unanswered
- The abstract does not disclose the study population or sample size — 以原文为准

## Consistency with other evidence

- Consistent with T66 (blue covarine was the best performer after the first brushing cycle, and the
  best after simulated continuous use) — but T66 also measured appearance, not stain removal
- Consistent with T70 (whitening **toothpaste** performed no better than conventional paste over 12
  weeks, while whitening **mouthrinses** and 10% carbamide peroxide did) — appearance effects do not
  accumulate
- The cluster's message: distinguish **optical whitening** (instant, reversible) from **chemical
  bleaching** (slower, real) from **abrasive stain removal** (modest, cumulative)
